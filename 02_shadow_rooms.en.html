The room of shadows and dividers

The closest answer (shadow), sorting sides (divider), the range of neighbors (ball), and matching units. At the end there's a nearest-neighbor Showdown.

By Ahn Sangsun (CEO, M-Robo Inc.) · About the author · 2026-11-19 · v1.4

New here? This page in 3 lines

  • What: Pick the least-wrong answer with a shadow, sort sides with a divider, and find neighbors after matching units.
  • Why it matters: Drawing prediction lines, spam filtering, and matching units before recommending are all rules of these rooms.
  • At work: You'll know how to pick "the answer closest to the truth among those a model can give" and "a fair distance."

No code or formulas required. This page deals only with real numbers (ordinary numbers).

Room 6

The shadow is the closest answer

The foot of a straight drop from a point to a line

Source card 06/10 · @eeanimationMethod: find the shadow (orthogonal projection)Everyday: a stick's shadow at noon

Why use this method

  1. Problem — When a model's possible answers lie only on one line, the true answer is usually off that line.
  2. Limit of the previous room — In Room 5 we read coordinates, but we don't know which point on the line is closest to the true answer.
  3. Solution — The foot of the perpendicular dropped from the true answer to the line (the shadow) is the closest point.
In plain words — Dropping at a slant makes a hypotenuse, which is always longer. The foot of a straight drop is the shortest.

The assistant, who finds math formulas hard, asks for you

Assistant They say this formula finds the closest point on a line (the shadow). proj_u(v) = (v·u ÷ u·u) u… There are two dots inside the fraction!

In school math terms — orthogonal projection, the foot of the perpendicular

Formula pieceMeaning
v·uOverlap score of v and u
u·uSquare of u's length
v·u ÷ u·uHow many times to stretch u to get the shadow
pShadow (the closest point)
r = v − pMiss — always at a right angle to u
Try it with numbers
v = (3, 2), u = (1, 0) → 3 ÷ 1 = 3 times → p = (3, 0), r = (0, 2), r·u = 0
u = (2, 1) → v·u = 8, u·u = 5 → p = 1.6 × (2, 1) = (3.2, 1.6), r = (−0.2, 0.4), r·u = 0
Heights 160, 162, 164 as one number: 162 gives a sum of squared errors of 4 + 0 + 4 = 8; 161 gives 1 + 1 + 9 = 11 → the average 162 is the shadow
Assistant The key is that the miss is always at a right angle. That's why it's the shortest.

In everyday terms

Stand a stick up at noon and its shadow falls right at its foot. The more slanted the sun, the longer the shadow.

In machine learning

Drawing a prediction line (least-squares regression) means dropping the true answer as a shadow onto "the answers the model can give." The average of several values is also a shadow.

Explanation

As the source concludes, the shadow is the closest point on the line through u.

In one sentence — If you measure a height 3 times and must sum it up in one number, the average of 162cm is the least-wrong answer.

This room's limit — The "floor" for dropping shadows was just one line. How do we handle a plane or a bigger floor, and a board that splits sides? → Room 7
As Python code from the course notes (optional)
p = (v @ u) / (u @ u) * u

Stretch u by the overlap score ratio to make the shadow.

Result: [3.2, 1.6] — same as the (3.2, 1.6) we got by hand.

Room 7

Lines, planes, dividers

A divider in an n-axis room is a board with one axis fewer

Source card 07/10 · @eeanimationMethod: sorting sides with the divider equationEveryday: the center line of a playground

Why use this method

  1. Problem — To separate spam from normal mail, you need to set up a divider in the room.
  2. Limit of the previous room — Room 6's floor was a single line. We need a wider floor and a board that splits sides.
  3. Solution — Decide "which side" by the overlap score with n, the arrow stuck into the divider (the normal).
In plain words — It's like splitting the blue team and the white team with one line down the middle of a playground.

The assistant, who finds math formulas hard, asks for you

Assistant They say this formula tells which side of the divider a point is on. n·(x − p) = 0, a hyperplane in ℝⁿ has n − 1 dimensions… n shows up with two meanings!

In school math terms — equations of lines and planes, distance between a point and a plane

Formula pieceMeaning
x = p + tvGoing t along direction v from start point p = a line
nThe arrow stuck into the divider (normal)
n·(x − p) = 0Every point on the divider is at a right angle to n
x₁ + 2x₂ + 2x₃ = 6The divider with n = (1, 2, 2)
n − 1A divider in a room with n axes has one axis fewer
Try it with numbers
Where it meets the axes: (6, 0, 0), (0, 3, 0), (0, 0, 3) — same as the source
Point (2, 2, 2): 2 + 4 + 4 = 10 > 6 → far side from the origin, distance to the divider (10 − 6) ÷ 3 ≈ 1.33
Origin: 0 < 6 → origin side, distance to the divider 6 ÷ 3 = 2 (length of n is √(1 + 4 + 4) = 3)
Assistant Just compare the score with 6 and you're done. A spam detector is simpler than I thought.

In everyday terms

Left of the playground line is the blue team, right is the white team. The farther from the line, the surer the side.

In machine learning

Classifiers like logistic regression and SVM set up a divider (decision boundary). The farther a point is from the divider, the more confident the call.

MoaWhich side is a point sitting right on the divider?
Fruit Fly ResearcherChange point P's three coordinates and find where the score is 6.
Lab panel 7The divider from source card 07, redrawn · Try changing the coordinates of point P

Explanation

If the score is above 6, it's on the far side from the origin; below, the origin side; equal, on the divider (boundary).

A point on the divider holds off on a call. Real classifiers also get most confused by points near the boundary.

A divider in a 3-axis room is a plane (2 axes). In an n-axis room, it's a board with n − 1 axes.

📝 Where we differ from the source — In the source, the axis name x and the point name x overlap (x = p + tv and the x-axis). On this page, the axes are renamed x₁, x₂, x₃, and the divider equation is written x₁ + 2x₂ + 2x₃ = 6. The source's "Hyperplane in ℝ³" is the same as a plane in a 3-axis room.

Room 8

Slide, rotate, or flip — distance stays the same

Moves that keep the shape and moves that change it

Source card 08/10 · @eeanimationMethod: pick moves that keep distancesEveryday: rotating a photo

Why use this method

  1. Problem — Even if you rotate a photo or move the origin of the coordinates, the relationships between data should stay the same.
  2. Limit of the previous room — Up to Room 7, we only measured points where they sat. We don't know what happens if they move.
  3. Solution — Sliding, rotating, and flipping keep distances and angles. Only stretching changes distances.
In plain words — A triangle on paper is the same triangle after you slide, rotate, or flip it. Only an enlarged copy changes size.

The assistant, who finds math formulas hard, asks for you

Assistant They say this formula is the rule for moving points without changing distances. x′ = Qx + t, QᵀQ = I… And what are the superscript T and the I?

In school math terms — translation, rotation, reflection, similarity

Formula pieceMeaning
tSlide — add the same arrow to every point
QRotate/flip table
QᵀQ = ICondition for the rotate/flip table: it doesn't change lengths or angles
Stretch by sEvery distance becomes |s| times
|s| = 1Only then does stretching also keep distances
Try it with numbers
Length 5 from (0, 0) to (3, 4) → rotate 90°, and it's length 5 from (0, 0) to (−4, 3)
Slide: add the same (−2, 1) to both points and the difference B − A stays the same → distance unchanged
Stretch by 2: length 5 → 10
Assistant So a rotation table means picking only tables that keep distances.

In everyday terms

Rotate a phone photo and the face is still the same face. Zoom in and only the size changes.

In machine learning

Rotating and flipping photos adds more training data (data augmentation). The shape stays the same, so the answer label stays the same too.

Room 9

Inside radius r = neighbors

Circles, spheres, balls — and what happens with many axes

Source card 09/10 · @eeanimationMethod: set the neighbor range with a radiusEveryday: within 1km of home

Why use this method

  1. Problem — Sometimes you want "all customers within distance r" rather than "a few nearby customers."
  2. Limit of the previous room — Up to Room 8, we only measured the distance between two points. We never drew a range.
  3. Solution — Gather the points within distance r of the center: in a 2-axis room you get a circle, in a 3-axis room a ball.
In plain words — Draw a 1km-radius circle around your home, and the stores inside are "neighborhood stores."

The assistant, who finds math formulas hard, asks for you

Assistant They say this formula sets the range up to distance r from the center. ‖x − c‖ = r and ‖x − c‖ ≤ r… One symbol of difference and the name changes?

In school math terms — equations of circles and spheres, exponents

Formula pieceMeaning
cCenter
‖x − c‖ = rPoints exactly r from the center = the outer shell (circle or sphere)
‖x − c‖ ≤ rEverything inside r = a solid ball
x² + y² = r²A circle centered at the origin
0.9ⁿShare of an n-axis ball's volume inside radius 0.9
Try it with numbers
Radius 1: circle area π × 1² ≈ 3.14, ball volume 4π ÷ 3 ≈ 4.19
Share inside radius 0.9: 2 axes 0.81 (81%), 3 axes 0.729 (73%)
10 axes 0.9¹⁰ ≈ 0.349 (35%), 100 axes 0.9¹⁰⁰ ≈ 0.0000266 — almost all in the outer shell
Assistant With many axes, the inside of the ball becomes almost empty. Fascinating.

In everyday terms

A delivery app's "stores within 2km" is this circle.

In machine learning

Used for radius-neighbor search and finding odd values (no neighbors within the radius is suspicious). With a huge number of axes, all points end up about equally far (the curse of dimensionality).

A bigger radius means more neighbors. If stores are spread evenly, the count grows in proportion to the circle area πr².

As you add axes, most of the volume moves to the outer shell. In a 100-axis ball, only 0.003% lies inside radius 0.9.

This room's limit — Distances measured with a straight ruler give wrong answers when units are mixed or the surface curves. → Room 10
As Python code from the course notes (optional)
np.sum(np.linalg.norm(X - c, axis=1) <= r)

Count the points within distance r of center c.

Result: you get the number of points within that radius.

Check quiz — Why does finding the "nearest neighbor" get hard in a room with a huge number of axes?
The volume crowds into the outer shell, so differences in distance shrink (the curse of dimensionality).The formula stays the same. The problem is that all the points crowd near the outer shell.
Room 10

Where this room is used and the limits of a straight ruler

Units must be matched, and on a curved surface a straight ruler measures short

Source card 10/10 · @eeanimationMethod: matching units, comparing arc and chordEveryday: airline routes

Why use this method

  1. Problem — Engineering (positions), graphics (rotation), and data analysis (nearest neighbors) all use this room's distance.
  2. Limits of the previous room — But up to Room 9, we assumed every axis uses the same unit.
  3. Solution — Match the units (feature scaling), and on curved surfaces, measure along the surface.
In plain words — Write height in mm and the height numbers grow 10×, taking over the distance on their own.

The assistant, who finds math formulas hard, asks for you

Assistant They say this formula compares the length measured along a curved surface with the straight-line length. d_surf = Rθ, d_Euc = 2R sin(θ/2)… Why does a sine suddenly show up?

In school math terms — unit conversion, arc length, trigonometric functions

Formula pieceMeaning
‖x − y‖₂Room 3's straight-ruler distance
y* = argminPick the y with the smallest distance (nearest neighbor)
RθLength along the surface (arc) — radius × central angle
2R sin(θ/2)Length of a straight tunnel through the ground (chord)
Try it with numbers
New customer (165cm, 55kg) vs Customer A (160, 56): in cm √(5² + 1²) ≈ 5.10, Customer B (166, 48): √(1² + 7²) ≈ 7.07 → nearest neighbor is A
Height in mm: A √(50² + 1²) ≈ 50.01, B √(10² + 7²) ≈ 12.21 → the neighbor switches to B
Earth R = 6,371km, central angle 90°: arc ≈ 10,008km, chord ≈ 9,010km
Assistant Same people, but one unit change switches the neighbor. That's scary.

In everyday terms

A flight from Seoul to New York doesn't follow a straight line on the map; it follows the Earth's surface.

In machine learning

Before nearest-neighbor search or clustering, match the units of each column (standardization). For data where direction matters, like word points, use Room 4's cosine instead of distance.

Explanation

Contrary to AbaBot, units do change distances. The source also notes that "feature scaling matters."

With a small central angle, arc and chord are nearly the same. The larger the angle, the shorter the straight ruler measures compared with the real path.

At 180°, the arc is 20,015km and the chord is the diameter, 12,742km.

The engineering (position, movement) axis of source card 10 was already covered in Room 2, and the graphics (rotate, shift) axis in Room 8.

In one sentence — Just switching height to mm changes the "most similar customer." Match units before you measure distance.

Check it

Ask whether the units of each column were matched. If height stays in mm, the height numbers take over the distance. Measured in cm, the answer changes to Customer A.

Limits of this room — The ruler and protractor of this room work only for data with a fixed number of axes. They can't hold data that runs on without end, like sound or sensor curves. → End of the corridor
As Python code from the course notes (optional)
(X - X.mean(axis=0)) / X.std(axis=0)

Subtract each column's mean and divide by its spread to match units (standardization).

Result: a table where every column has mean 0 and spread 1.

Referee's verdict

Sia's way measures height gaps in mm, making them big, like 30, 10, 50. The weight gaps of 2, 6, 1kg get almost buried. So it picked Customer E, who is close only in height.

Moa's "5cm·5kg" means these customers' heights and weights usually spread by about that much (example values). Using the spread as one unit makes the two columns fair. Customer D is the closest, at distance 0.72.

In practice, this "spread" is calculated directly from the data. The standardization in Room 10's code box does exactly that.

It's Room 10's lesson exactly. If you don't match units, the column with big numbers takes over the distance.

The endless room at the end of the hallway

The next part is Hilbert space. We won't teach it here; we'll only introduce what it is and why it's needed.

① Today's rooms in review

We measured how far apart things are with a ruler (distance), how alike they are with a protractor (overlap score), and where the closest answer is with a shadow.

② What this room can't do

The number of axes has to be fixed. It can't hold data that runs on without end, like sound, sensor curves, or stock price movements.

Even one second of music, written down at CD quality, is 44100 numbers. Yet the sound curve itself goes on without end.

③ What Hilbert space is

  • Keep the ruler and protractor, and widen the room without end.
  • Treat one curve as one point, and measure "how far, how alike, and the shadow" between curves too.
  • The rules you learned today work almost unchanged. The only thing that changes is the number of axes.
